## Dossier S12 / Commission C05: From draft to final labour rules

**Historical sequence:** On 21 November 2025, the Industrial Relations Code provisions were brought into force. On **30 December 2025**, the Ministry of Labour and Employment published **draft** Industrial Relations (Central) Rules, 2025 (G.S.R. 930(E)) for objections and suggestions. On **8 May 2026**, it promulgated the **Industrial Relations (Central) Rules, 2026** (G.S.R. 342(E)), following consideration of comments. The final rule's commencement clause states it comes into force on Gazette publication. Do not conflate the date the Code commenced with the date the subordinate Central Rules were finalized.

**Primary documents:**
- Draft Central Rules, 30 Dec 2025, Ministry of Labour and Employment: https://www.labour.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/01/0ea4e7d1b8f264a8cd27bb4560e9202c.pdf
- Final Central Rules, 8 May 2026, Ministry of Labour and Employment: https://www.labour.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/05/f05a2c220dcdec0ea9c55e84d9ff791f.pdf
- The Industrial Relations Code, 2020 and Gazette commencement notice S.O. 5320(E), 21 Nov 2025, referenced in the draft's official preamble. Obtain and cite the actual commencement Gazette.

**Article question:** *What did employers, unions, workers and government gain or lose between the draft and the final notified central rules?*

**Required evidence workflow:**
1. Build a clause-by-clause crosswalk, preserving the draft's clause number, final rule number, subject, original language, final language and significance. No legal conclusion from a snippet; inspect the full Gazette text.
2. Separately record the Code's enabling sections, the Central Rules, and **applicable State Rules**. A central rule's presence does not establish universal coverage of every establishment in every state.
3. Focus on industrial dispute procedure, standing orders, works committees/grievance processes, notice and consultation, reporting and electronic forms where supported by exact provisions.
4. Investigate any transitional clauses and the rules that were superseded.
5. Identify measurable outcomes: registration/filing times, disputes resolved, settlement rates, time to adjudication, worker participation and employer compliance cost. Distinguish announced rights from observed enforcement.
6. Compare claims from trade unions, employer bodies and administration against the operative text, clearly distinguishing stakeholder forecasts from verified effects.

**Suggested visual:** 4-column dated comparison: provision, draft text, final text, who is affected. Include links to Gazette paragraph/page. Complement with a one-page lifecycle diagram from Code commencement to publication and implementation.

**Disqualifying shortcuts:** Publishing the December draft as final law; assuming the May central rule automatically covers all states; claiming that a specific number of jobs was created or lost without credible data; treating an objection period as a completed implementation milestone.

**Next output:** 1,500–2,000-word historical investigation with direct Gazette citations, a downloadable machine-readable draft–final comparison and an identified policy-outcome test.

## Dossier S14 / Commission C06: Did the Economic Survey's capital-spending case survive the year?

**Historical event:** **29 January 2026**, Economic Survey 2025–26 tabled in Parliament. Its investment/infrastructure chapter and accompanying official communications argued that sustained public capital expenditure strengthens infrastructure and private investment conditions. It reported FY2026 Budget Estimate Union capex of **₹11.21 lakh crore** and effective capital expenditure of **₹15.48 lakh crore**. Both are **Budget Estimates**, not audited final FY26 outturns. <https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2219991&lang=2&reg=3>

**Primary documents:**
- Government Economic Survey 2025–26 full source index and statistical appendix: https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/
- Infrastructure chapter 9: https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/economicsurvey/doc/eschapter/echap09.pdf
- Government 29 Jan 2026 fiscal and capital expenditure release: https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2220005&lang=2&reg=48
- Government infrastructure chapter highlights: https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2219991&lang=2&reg=3
- Monthly Union expenditure by Controller General of Accounts and subsequent FY26 provisional/actual figures. Retrieve exact release and period at drafting time rather than inserting a placeholder number as a result.
- Subsequent MoSPI national accounts capital-formation data and corresponding investment revisions; preserve base and release vintage.

**Article question:** *Did the public investment commitments documented in January become timely construction, installed capacity, reliable services and private follow-on investment?*

**Work plan:**
1. Build a **vintage-consistent** table for FY2018–FY2026: nominal Union capital expenditure (Budget Estimates, Revised Estimates and actuals where available), effective capex (including grants to create capital assets, distinctly labelled), debt service and fiscal balance. Never compare an actual against a budgeted figure as though both were realizations.
2. Reconcile the FY26 ₹11.21 lakh crore capex estimate with **later provisional and final outturns** and financing. Distinguish cash spending and loan transfers from the destination project's physical completion.
3. Use the Scheme for Special Assistance to States for Capital Investment (SASCI) carefully: disbursement from the Centre does not automatically equal completed State-level infrastructure.
4. Test one concrete mechanism (e.g., port turnaround, logistics time, power capacity utilisation or repeatable rail freight throughput) with independently recorded outcomes rather than applying an unqualified multipliers-to-GDP number.
5. Separate Gross Fixed Capital Formation growth from **private corporate** investment. Look for listed-company realised capex, capacity utilisation, tender awards and commissioning, with sample limitations.
6. Evaluate the Survey's growth thesis against outcomes available by 14 November 2026, explicitly noting what still cannot be known.

**Suggested visual:** Three-track project scorecard: **Budgeted → Released → Spent → Commissioned → Utilized**. Add one transparent chart of budget versus outturn; a separate private-capex hypothesis table.

**Disqualifying shortcuts:** Calling an FY26 BE figure actual spending; treating effective capex as identical to direct Union capex; claiming that a multiplier estimate directly predicts realized GDP; calling all GFCF “private investment”.

**Next output:** 1,500–2,000-word retrospective with quantified budget-to-outturn differences where verified, project implementation evidence and clear constraints on causal attribution.
