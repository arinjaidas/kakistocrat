# The Kakistocrat Weekly Retrospective

## A year of oil, the rupee and trade

**52 weekly articles · 14 November 2025 to 6 November 2026 · Written on 11 November 2026**

Each article covers a completed trading week. The date above each piece is its historical coverage date. The whole series was prepared retrospectively and uses the sources available at compilation, including later releases where they clarify a development.

### How to read the data

The U.S. Energy Information Administration reports weekly Europe Brent spot prices in dollars per barrel. USD/INR is the average of the available Federal Reserve H.10 New York noon observations in the trading week, rounded to two decimals for display. A higher USD/INR value means a weaker rupee. Translated Brent multiplies the two weekly averages before rounding. It is a benchmark for exposure, separate from the price, grade, freight, insurance and hedges of an actual cargo. The week ending 6 November had no EIA weekly spot observation available at compilation. Its article uses the separately identified Brent futures settlement and does not compute a spot-based rupee proxy.

## 14 November 2025 · Cheaper Brent reached India almost intact

*Week 1 · Historical coverage date. Written retrospectively on 11 November 2026.*

The first week in this series offers a clean baseline. Brent fell from $64.30 to $62.87 a barrel, while the rupee's weekly average barely moved. The rupee value of a benchmark barrel fell about 2.2%, close to the drop in its dollar price. For an importer facing the benchmark and buying dollars at the observed rate, nearly all of the weekly price relief came through the commodity.

That is the starting point for separating two shocks that are often bundled together. A refinery may price a cargo off a different grade, agree to a delivery-period average, or hedge both legs. Those details determine the invoice. The useful question for India's external accounts is whether softer prices persist long enough to reduce payments on actual cargoes. A week of cheap spot oil can ease expectations before it changes the monthly import bill.

## 21 November 2025 · Why the dollar matters even when the oil comes from elsewhere

*Week 2 · Historical coverage date. Written retrospectively on 11 November 2026.*

Brent recovered to $63.64 and the rupee slipped slightly to 88.80 per dollar. The implied rupee cost of benchmark crude rose 1.4%. In a week with modest moves on both sides, the interesting distinction is between where India sources crude and which currency its refiners need to pay for it.

Diversification can protect a refinery against disruption at a particular port or supplier. It gives much less protection against an expensive settlement currency when replacement cargoes carry dollar-linked prices too. Refiners can arrange forward currency purchases, adjust crude blends, or negotiate payment terms. Each choice shifts the timing or distribution of risk. Measuring the effect of a sourcing change requires grade-level import prices and quantities, while measuring the currency leg requires the exchange rate at settlement or the firm's hedge rate. The two sets of data answer different questions.

## 28 November 2025 · When oil and the rupee move against importers together

*Week 3 · Historical coverage date. Written retrospectively on 11 November 2026.*

The November close brought a small example of a double squeeze. Brent rose to $64.38 and the rupee weakened to a weekly average of 89.27 per dollar. The rupee value of benchmark Brent rose by about 1.7%, faster than the dollar price. That is how two ordinary market moves can produce a more consequential increase for a company that earns rupees and buys fuel priced in dollars.

A quick accounting check is to translate the crude price before comparing weeks. At a fixed exchange rate the rise would have been about 1.2%. The currency move added roughly half a percentage point. This arithmetic explains a price exposure, while it takes more evidence to explain why the rupee fell. Portfolio flows, dollar demand from other importers, and movements in other emerging-market currencies would be useful checks before making a causal claim.

## 5 December 2025 · The RBI cut rates as the oil benefit faded in rupees

*Week 4 · Historical coverage date. Written retrospectively on 11 November 2026.*

On 5 December, the Reserve Bank of India's monetary policy committee reduced the repo rate to 5.25%. In the same week Brent slipped 0.6%, while USD/INR rose 0.7%. At the average rates, benchmark oil cost almost the same in rupees as a week earlier. The oil headline suggested some relief that the exchange rate largely absorbed.

These developments illustrate the different horizons a central bank faces. Lower policy rates can support borrowing and domestic demand over months. An exchange-rate move can alter an importer's next dollar purchase immediately, although hedges and contract schedules determine when it shows up in costs. To judge the policy stance, the useful comparison is between the central bank's inflation forecast and the price pressures that persist after this week's oil and FX moves. A rate cut and a weaker currency in the same week alone establish no cause and effect.

## 12 December 2025 · A Fed cut and a softer rupee can coexist

*Week 5 · Historical coverage date. Written retrospectively on 11 November 2026.*

On 10 December, the Federal Reserve cut its target range by a quarter point to 3.50%–3.75%. Brent fell to $62.60 over the week, while the weekly rupee average moved from 89.92 to 90.15 per dollar. That combination is a reminder that an announced U.S. rate cut and the returns investors expect to earn from holding dollars are different observations.

A cut that markets have anticipated can already be reflected in bond and currency prices. What matters for the exchange rate is often the revision to the expected path of rates, together with changes in risk appetite and the cost of hedging rupee assets. A better event study would compare the release-time moves in U.S. two-year yields, the broad dollar and the rupee, and match the announcement to the next available Indian trading window. The weekly average alone cannot show that sequence.

## 19 December 2025 · Cheaper oil offers relief before the trade data arrive

*Week 6 · Historical coverage date. Written retrospectively on 11 November 2026.*

Brent's weekly average fell to $60.83, down almost 5% from the first week of December. The rupee was weaker over that stretch, yet the oil decline was large enough to push the benchmark value of a barrel down to roughly ₹5,498. At constant quantities, a lower benchmark price should eventually ease dollar demand from buyers paying market-linked cargo prices.

The delay matters. Import figures record transactions over a period, while market data tell us what a fresh transaction could cost today. Shipments priced earlier may arrive during a week when the market is already cheaper. Some buyers may also increase quantities if they think the low price is temporary. To distinguish a genuine terms-of-trade gain from an inventory decision, compare crude import volumes and unit values. A drop in the value of imports with steady volumes tells a different story from a drop driven by fewer barrels.

## 26 December 2025 · The holiday week that exposed a currency buffer

*Week 7 · Historical coverage date. Written retrospectively on 11 November 2026.*

Brent rose almost 4% in the week ending 26 December, while the rupee strengthened on the available Federal Reserve quotes. The translated benchmark rose about 3.2%. The exchange-rate move absorbed part of the oil rebound and provided a small buffer for an importer buying both at those rates.

Holiday weeks make this kind of comparison easy to overread. The New York exchange-rate average uses four observations, and thin trading or irregular execution can affect what an individual firm pays. An importer that had bought its dollars the previous week would see a different offset altogether. The practical lesson is to distinguish the exposures of the underlying cargo and the hedge. A rupee-based margin model can simulate both prices moving, while a treasury desk also needs the dates and rates already locked into its positions. One average helps describe the week and leaves that timing question open.

## 2 January 2026 · What one week around New Year can tell us about 2026

*Week 8 · Historical coverage date. Written retrospectively on 11 November 2026.*

The week ending 2 January put Brent at $62.18 and the rupee at 89.94 per dollar on the available observations. A simple multiplication gives a benchmark of about ₹5,593 per barrel. That is a useful reference for the year because it tells an Indian buyer what both market prices looked like before either had made a large move in 2026.

Calendar boundaries can obscure the comparison. The week includes trading days in December as well as January, and a financial-year projection spans a different interval. The sensible use of this price is as a scenario baseline. Hold volumes constant and ask what a 10% change in Brent, a 5% depreciation, or both would mean in rupees. Then make separate assumptions about freight and import volumes. The exercise sets out where a budget forecast is vulnerable without treating a holiday-week quote as a forecast.

## 9 January 2026 · Crude futures, spot prices and the bills refiners pay

*Week 9 · Historical coverage date. Written retrospectively on 11 November 2026.*

Brent spot averaged $62.93 and USD/INR averaged 90.11 during the first full trading week of January. The modest increase in the translated benchmark offers a reason to ask which oil price belongs in an Indian cost model. A futures headline describes a contract for a specific delivery month. Spot Brent is a benchmark for prompt physical crude. An actual refinery purchase may use either as part of a formula and include a premium or discount for a specific grade.

The distinctions become important when the market expects supply to change. A nearby cargo can be expensive even while a later futures price is lower, making timing and inventory worth money. For an economy-wide assessment, weekly spot Brent is a consistent starting point. A procurement estimate needs the mix of imported grades, the contract pricing window, freight and the exchange rate the buyer can actually secure.

## 16 January 2026 · The January oil rebound made the fuel-cost sensitivity visible

*Week 10 · Historical coverage date. Written retrospectively on 11 November 2026.*

Brent jumped from $62.93 to $67.00, a weekly increase of about 6.5%. The rupee weakened only modestly, so the translated benchmark rose about 6.7%. In this case the commodity leg did nearly all of the work. A company monitoring only the exchange rate would have missed the larger change in its exposure.

The incidence of that move varies across the economy. A carrier buying fuel may feel the increase through its operating costs. A refinery's margin depends on how the value of its products moves relative to the cost of crude. A consumer's experience depends on pricing decisions and tax treatment, which can change the speed or extent of pass-through. To track the shock, start with the dollar benchmark, translate it into rupees, and then follow wholesale product prices and retail prices separately. That sequence avoids assuming that a barrel and a litre move together.

## 23 January 2026 · Flat Brent, higher rupee costs

*Week 11 · Historical coverage date. Written retrospectively on 11 November 2026.*

At $66.99, Brent was effectively unchanged from the week before. The rupee averaged 91.56 per dollar, compared with 90.34 the previous week. That currency move lifted the implied rupee cost of a barrel by roughly 1.3%. A commodity trader following dollars could reasonably have called oil flat. An unhedged Indian buyer saw a higher replacement cost.

This is a useful counterexample to the idea that energy inflation always begins with a commodity rally. Currency depreciation can raise the local price of oil, machinery and other dollar-priced inputs together. An exporter with dollar receipts may offset some of that exposure, depending on its input mix and payment schedule. A domestic firm without foreign earnings has a different balance sheet. Before interpreting the effect on corporate margins, split firms by their net dollar exposure. Export status alone is too coarse a measure.

## 30 January 2026 · The EU trade deal and the oil bill run on different clocks
*Week 12 · Historical coverage date. Written retrospectively on 11 November 2026.*

On 27 January the European Union and India announced the conclusion of negotiations for a free trade agreement. That week Brent averaged $70.43 and the rupee averaged 91.88 per dollar. The oil move could affect the cost of fresh imports immediately. The trade announcement concerned market access that would work through later steps, implementation and decisions by firms.

The difference matters when evaluating India's external position. A producer considering more exports to Europe needs the tariff schedule for its product, its rules of origin and any compliance costs. Even where access improves, expanding capacity or finding a buyer can take time. Oil bills respond much faster to commodity prices. A useful assessment tracks the two channels separately: near-term crude costs and a longer-run change in export opportunities. The exchange rate can respond to expectations about both, which makes the timing of the underlying evidence all the more important.

## 13 February 2026 · A tariff preference is useful only if firms can claim it

*Week 14 · Historical coverage date. Written retrospectively on 11 November 2026.*

The week after the U.S.–India framework, Brent averaged $70.70 and the rupee was little changed on the weekly average. A quieter currency market gave exporters a different question to consider: how much of a negotiated tariff preference would reach an actual shipment?

A factory needs to establish where its goods originate under the applicable rules. It may have to document where intermediate inputs came from and what processing occurred in India. If verifying origin is expensive, or if the preference over the ordinary tariff is small, some firms may choose to enter at the ordinary rate. Larger exporters can spread the documentation cost across more orders. A serious evaluation of a trade deal therefore needs preference-utilisation data after implementation. An announced rate tells us what an eligible shipment can claim. Utilisation tells us how many firms find it worthwhile to claim it.

## 20 February 2026 · Services receipts change the meaning of India's trade gap

*Week 15 · Historical coverage date. Written retrospectively on 11 November 2026.*

India's Commerce Ministry published its January trade estimates on 16 February. Its headline combined merchandise and services exports, with the January services figure explicitly identified as an estimate. Brent was also slightly dearer that week. Reading the two developments together shows why a higher oil bill is only one part of India's external position.

For a clean monthly comparison, separate actual merchandise observations from services estimates, then check how the picture changes once the RBI publishes quarterly balance-of-payments data. A headline that mixes the two is useful as a quick gauge, but its components have different vintages and different implications for demand for dollars.

## 13 March 2026 · What an emergency oil release buys is time

On 11 March the International Energy Agency announced that its member countries would make 400 million barrels from emergency reserves available. Brent still averaged $96.16 that week, up from $85.28. The policy announcement and the price increase can coexist. An emergency release adds a source of barrels while the market is still trying to work out how much production and shipping it may lose.

The relevant comparison is between the rate at which stocks reach buyers and the rate at which supplies have been disrupted. Timing, location and crude grade also matter. A stock release can bridge a temporary gap if refiners can use the barrels and if transport routes are available. A persistent outage eventually requires a more durable adjustment in production, demand or sourcing. The published size of the release sets the scale. Delivery and inventory data show how much breathing room it actually provides.

## 20 March 2026 · Three rising weeks turned a crude shock into a cash-flow problem

*Week 19 · Historical coverage date. Written retrospectively on 11 November 2026.*

Brent averaged $111.40 in the week ending 20 March, after $85.28 and $96.16 in the previous two weeks. The rupee also weakened. At observed weekly averages, the translated benchmark rose to more than ₹10,300 a barrel, compared with about ₹6,490 in the last week of February.

That difference matters for working capital as well as for margins. A buyer financing the same volume of crude at a higher price may need a larger credit line, even if a later retail or wholesale price adjustment recovers some of the cost. Delayed deliveries can extend the period for which the cargo is financed. Exposure is therefore largest where cash goes out quickly and proceeds come in slowly.
